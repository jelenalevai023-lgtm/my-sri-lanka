Know before you go.

A few practical things are worth knowing before you travel to Sri Lanka. Nothing complicated — just the kind of small details that can make your trip easier and more enjoyable.

Travelling solo

Sri Lanka can be a wonderful destination for solo travellers.

I travelled around Sri Lanka entirely on my own and met many other people travelling solo along the way. What stayed with me most was how kind and welcoming the local people were. I never felt lonely or unwelcome during my trip.

It was easy to meet people, have a conversation or simply share a moment with someone along the way.

Of course, travelling alone means using common sense and paying attention to your surroundings, just as you would anywhere else. But you don’t need to be part of a group to enjoy Sri Lanka.

If you’ve been thinking about travelling solo, don’t let that stop you. You can explore the island independently while still feeling connected to the people and places around you.

Staying connected

Reliable internet made travelling around Sri Lanka much easier for me.

I was connected throughout my trip, which meant I could use maps, check information, contact accommodation, arrange transport and stay in touch with people at home whenever I needed to.

If you want to stay connected, you can get a local SIM or eSIM. Tourist SIMs are available at the airport, and several local providers offer prepaid tourist plans.

If your phone supports eSIM, you can also arrange one before or during your trip.

For a solo traveller in particular, having mobile internet is reassuring. It gives you the freedom to find your way around, make arrangements as you go and stay in contact when you need to.

Keep some cash with you

Even if you mostly use cards, it is useful to have some Sri Lankan rupees with you.

Smaller shops, local cafés, tuk-tuks and other everyday situations may be easier with cash, especially outside larger tourist areas.

And don’t worry about carrying only large notes. Having some smaller denominations can make paying for small purchases much easier.

You don’t need to carry a lot of cash at once — just enough for the places and situations where a card may not be convenient.

Electricity & charging

Sri Lanka uses 230V electricity at 50Hz.

Socket types can vary, so bringing a universal travel adapter is a good idea. Most modern phone and laptop chargers support a wide voltage range, but it is always worth checking your device before travelling.

If you’re travelling with several devices, a small multi-port charger can also save space and keep everything in one place.

Water

Bottled drinking water is the simplest choice for most visitors.

Keep a bottle with you when you’re out exploring, especially on hot days or when you’re travelling between destinations.

If you’re staying somewhere outside the usual tourist areas, simply ask your accommodation about the local water supply if you’re unsure.

A little patience goes a
long way

Sri Lanka has its own rhythm.

Things don’t always happen at exactly the pace you may be used to at home. A journey can take longer than expected, a meal may take a little more time, and plans can occasionally change.

Try not to let that frustrate you.

Leave some space in your itinerary, allow extra time when travelling between destinations and remember that you’re on holiday.

Sometimes slowing down is part of the experience.

Leave room for the unexpected

Not everything worth experiencing in Sri Lanka will be on your itinerary.

A conversation with a local, an unexpected stop, a beautiful view from a train window, a little café you didn’t plan to visit or a road that takes you somewhere you hadn’t intended to go can become one of your favourite memories.

You don’t have to plan every minute of your trip.

Leave a little room for the unexpected.

Some of the moments you remember most may be the ones you never planned for.

Before you go

Sri Lanka is easy to explore independently, but a little preparation can make your trip much smoother.

Check your entry requirements, have your important travel documents ready, make sure you have a way to stay connected and give yourself enough time between destinations.

Then leave some space for the rest.

The best parts of a trip aren’t always the ones you planned.
