Horton Plains:
into the mist.

Horton Plains feels like a completely different side of Sri Lanka. High in the central highlands, this national park is known for its cool air, open grasslands, cloud forests and trails that often disappear into the mist.

Where is Horton Plains?

Horton Plains National Park is located in Sri Lanka’s central highlands, near Nuwara Eliya. Sitting more than 2,000 metres above sea level, the park feels very different from the tropical beaches and hot lowlands most people associate with Sri Lanka.

Here, open grasslands meet cloud forests, the air is noticeably cooler, and mist can quickly change the view around you.

Horton Plains is best known for its scenic hiking trail, which takes visitors through some of the most distinctive landscapes in the country.

Why visit Horton Plains?

If you enjoy hiking and nature, Horton Plains is a great change of scenery.

This is not a safari-style national park where you drive around looking for wildlife. The main experience is the walk itself — following the trail through grasslands and misty forests, with several highlights along the way.

The cooler temperatures are also a welcome break from Sri Lanka’s tropical heat.

What to see

World’s End

The most famous viewpoint in Horton Plains is World’s End, a dramatic cliff where the land suddenly drops away.

On a clear day, the views can stretch far into the distance. But this is also where timing matters — once the mist rolls in, the entire view can disappear.

That’s why an early start is always
a good idea.

Baker’s Falls

Baker’s Falls is another highlight along the trail. Surrounded by lush vegetation, it makes a beautiful stop during the hike and adds a completely different landscape to
the open grasslands.

Wildlife

You might spot wildlife along the way, including Sri Lankan sambar deer and different species of birds. But don’t visit expecting the kind of wildlife experience you would get in Yala or Udawalawe.

Here, the landscapes are the real stars.

What to know before you go

The main circular trail is around 9–10 km long and usually takes about 3–4 hours

to complete.

The weather can be unpredictable, with cool mornings, rain and mist all possible, so wearing layers is a good idea. Comfortable walking shoes are also important, as parts of the trail can be
uneven or muddy.

Bring water and something to eat, especially if you’re planning to spend
several hours walking.

Tip

Start early. Horton Plains is beautiful in the mist, but if you want the best chance of seeing the view from World’s End, it’s worth getting
there in the morning.

How to get to Horton Plains

Horton Plains is most easily reached from Nuwara Eliya, although it can also be visited from Haputale or nearby parts of Sri Lanka’s hill country.

Most visitors arrange a private driver or taxi for an early morning departure. The drive itself can take time, so starting before sunrise is common if you want to reach the park early enough for the best
chance of clear views.
