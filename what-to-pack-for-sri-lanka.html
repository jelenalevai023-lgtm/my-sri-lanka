What to pack for Sri Lanka: what you’ll

really need

Packing for Sri Lanka can be
surprisingly difficult.

Not because you need a lot, but because you need very different things depending on where you are going.

You might spend one day sweating on the coast, the next walking through an ancient temple, and a few days later find yourself somewhere in the hills where the evenings are cool and the weather can change quickly.

The good news is that you do not need to pack your entire wardrobe.

You need a few things that work hard, dry quickly, keep you comfortable, and can handle a little bit of everything.

Here is what I would actually put
in my suitcase.

Pack for heat, but do not forget the hills

For most of Sri Lanka, lightweight clothing is your friend.

Loose trousers, light dresses, cotton tops, T shirts and shorts are much more useful than anything heavy or complicated. Natural fabrics can feel particularly comfortable in the heat, although quick drying clothes can be incredibly useful when humidity
is high.

But do not make the mistake of thinking that the whole island is hot in exactly the same way.

The central highlands are cooler, especially in the evenings. If your trip includes places such as Nuwara Eliya or other higher areas, pack one light jumper,
cardigan or jacket.

You will probably not wear it every day.

You will be very happy to have it when you need it.

Bring something you can throw over your shoulders

This is one of those small things that can make a big difference.

Sri Lanka is a tropical destination, but you will also visit places where beachwear is
simply not appropriate.

Temples and other religious sites require more modest clothing, and you may need to cover your shoulders and legs. Shoes also need to be removed at
many religious sites.

A lightweight scarf or shawl takes almost no room in your bag and can become one of the most useful things you pack.

It can cover your shoulders.

It can protect you from the sun.

It can keep you warm on a cool evening.

And it can save you from standing outside a temple wondering what you are supposed to do with the tiny top you thought was perfectly reasonable
five minutes earlier.

Shoes matter more than
you think

You do not need five different pairs of shoes.

You need shoes you can actually walk in.

A comfortable pair of trainers or walking shoes is probably the most important pair you can bring. Ancient sites, railway stations, markets, streets, viewpoints and long days of exploring can all involve considerably more walking than you expect.

A pair of sandals or flip flops is useful for beaches, guesthouses and easy days.

If you are planning proper hikes, choose footwear for the terrain rather than assuming your everyday trainers will be enough.

And remember that religious sites often require you to remove your shoes. Comfortable footwear that is easy to take on and off is therefore a small luxury
in its own right.

A light rain layer is
worth the space

Sri Lanka has two monsoon systems and weather can vary considerably depending on which part of the island you are visiting and when you go.

That does not mean you need to carry a giant waterproof jacket everywhere.

A small umbrella or lightweight rain jacket can be much more practical.

A rain cover for your day bag is also worth considering, particularly if you carry a camera, phone, notebook or anything else you really do not want soaked.

And there is another reason to think
about rain.

Things do not always dry quickly in
humid weather.

So packing a few lightweight, quick drying clothes can make your life considerably easier than bringing lots of
heavier cotton pieces.

Your day bag will probably work harder
than your suitcase

Once you arrive, you will spend most days carrying a much smaller bag.

Choose one that is comfortable, light and easy to keep close to you.

Mine would have room for:

A reusable water bottle.

Sunscreen.

A small amount of cash.

Your phone.

A power bank.

Your scarf or cover up.

A small umbrella or rain layer.

And perhaps a plastic or waterproof pouch for anything that needs protecting from rain or water.

You do not need to carry your entire suitcase around Sri Lanka.

In fact, you really do not want to.

Sun protection is not optional

Sri Lanka is close to the equator, and the combination of strong sun, heat and humidity can catch you out even when the day does not feel particularly hot.

Bring sunscreen that you are comfortable using generously.

A hat is useful.

Sunglasses are useful.

And a lightweight shirt can sometimes be more comfortable than constantly trying to hide from the sun with your hands.

Do not rely on the fact that you are spending the day somewhere cloudy either. The weather in Sri Lanka can change quickly, particularly in the hills.

A power bank can save your day

Your phone will probably become one of your most useful travel tools.

Maps.

Tickets.

Booking confirmations.

Photos.

Translation.

Messages.

Restaurant searches.

Directions back to your accommodation after you have confidently walked off in completely the wrong direction.

A small power bank is therefore one of those things that is easy to forget and
annoying to be without.

Also pack the appropriate travel adapter for your devices.

Sri Lanka uses 230 to 240 volts and 50 Hz electricity, so check that your devices and chargers are compatible before you travel.

Keep a small waterproof pouch

This is one of those wonderfully boring travel items that becomes useful constantly.

Use it for things such as:

Your passport when you are travelling between places.

Cash.

Cards.

A phone at the beach.

Wet swimwear.

Small electronics.

Important papers.

You do not need anything fancy.

A simple waterproof pouch or zip bag can do the job.

What about toiletries?

Do not pack your entire bathroom.

Take the things you genuinely prefer to have with you, particularly if you use specific products that may be difficult to replace while travelling.

For everything else, keep it simple.

Travel size toiletries save space, and solid products can be particularly convenient if you are trying to keep your luggage light.

One thing worth remembering is that Sri Lanka is humid.

Products that feel perfectly normal at home can suddenly become much less pleasant when you are carrying them around in tropical heat.

Lightweight is usually better.

What I would not
bother packing

This is just as important as knowing
what to take.

You probably do not need:

A different outfit for every single day.

Several pairs of heavy shoes.

A huge collection of beauty products.

Bulky towels unless you have
a specific reason.

Lots of formal clothing.

Heavy jackets if you are staying mostly
on the coast.

A suitcase full of things you might
possibly need.

Sri Lanka is not a place where you need to dress differently every day to look like you have
made an effort.

Comfort wins.

You will be walking.

You will be sweating.

You may get caught in rain.

You may sit on a train for hours.

You may end up eating somewhere you never planned to visit.

The clothes you reach for again and again will be the comfortable ones.

One more thing: leave
some space

This is perhaps the least practical piece of packing advice, but one of my favourites.

Leave some room.

Sri Lanka is full of things you might want to bring home.

Tea.

Spices.

Small handmade pieces.

Clothes.

Art.

Little things you find in markets that you absolutely did not intend to buy.

You do not want to spend the last evening of your trip sitting on your suitcase
trying to make it close.

Leave a little space from the beginning.

You will probably find a reason to use it.

The simple Sri Lanka
packing list

If you want to keep things uncomplicated, start with this.

For clothing, lightweight tops and T shirts, loose trousers or skirts, shorts and beachwear, one light layer for cooler evenings, one modest outfit or cover up for temples, underwear and socks, and swimwear will cover most of your days.

For shoes, comfortable walking shoes and a pair of sandals or flip flops will get you through almost everything. Only pack proper hiking shoes if your plans
actually require them.

For weather and sun, sunscreen, sunglasses, a hat, a small umbrella or lightweight rain jacket, and a lightweight scarf or shawl will handle most of what Sri Lanka throws at you.

And a few useful extras are worth the space: a day bag, a reusable water bottle, a power bank, a travel adapter, a waterproof pouch, a small first aid kit, and copies of your important documents.

And then stop.

You do not need to pack for every possible version of your trip.

Pack for the trip you are actually
going to have.

Sri Lanka rewards light luggage, comfortable clothes and a little bit of flexibility.

And if you forget something?

There is a very good chance that the island will teach you what
you actually needed.
