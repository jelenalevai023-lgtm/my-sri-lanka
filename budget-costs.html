Budget & Costs in
Sri Lanka.

How much does it cost to travel around Sri Lanka?

The short answer is: it depends.

Sri Lanka can be a very affordable destination, but your daily spending will depend on where you stay, how you travel, where you eat and how many activities and attractions you include.

A backpacker travelling by bus and train, eating local food and staying in guesthouses will have a very different budget from someone travelling between boutique hotels with a private driver.

The good news is that Sri Lanka can work for a wide range of budgets.

How much should you budget per day?

As a general guide, you can think in terms of:

  • Budget travel: around US$30–50 per person per day
  • Mid-range travel: around US$60–120 per person per day
  • More comfortable travel: around US$120–200+ per person per day

These are broad estimates rather than fixed prices. They generally refer to your spending in Sri Lanka and do not include international flights.

Your actual costs can be lower or considerably higher depending on your choices.

Accommodation

Accommodation is one of the biggest factors affecting your travel budget.

You can find everything from simple guesthouses and hostels to boutique hotels, villas and high-end resorts.

If you are travelling on a budget, locally owned guesthouses can offer excellent value. Moving into the mid-range category gives you more comfort and often beautiful locations without necessarily becoming expensive by international standards.

At the higher end, beach resorts, private villas and boutique properties can increase your daily spending considerably.

Prices also vary by location and season. A room in a popular beach destination during peak season may cost considerably more than the same type of accommodation during a quieter period.

Food

Food is one of the easiest areas to adjust according to your budget.

Eating local food can be very affordable. Rice and curry, kottu, roti and other everyday Sri Lankan dishes are generally much cheaper than meals in restaurants aimed primarily at international visitors.

If you choose cafés, Western-style restaurants, hotel restaurants or more upscale dining, your food budget will naturally increase.

You don’t have to choose one or the other, either. Mixing local restaurants with the occasional nicer meal can be a good way to enjoy both Sri Lankan food and the places you discover along the way.

Getting around

Transport costs can vary enormously depending on how you travel.

Trains and public buses are among the most affordable options. Tuk-tuks are convenient for shorter journeys, while taxis and private drivers cost more but can save considerable time and effort.

If you are travelling between several destinations, a private driver can become one of the larger parts of your transport budget.

Domestic flights are another option when time matters more than price.

For a detailed look at all your transport options, see our Getting Around Sri Lanka guide.

Entrance fees and activities

This is one category that is easy to underestimate when planning a trip.

Sri Lanka has many beautiful places that are free or inexpensive to visit, but some of the country’s major cultural attractions and national parks have separate entrance fees for foreign visitors.

For example, the current official Central Cultural Fund rates list:

  • Sigiriya Rock Fortress — US$35
  • Polonnaruwa — US$30
  • Anuradhapura — US$30

These are current rates and can change, so check the official information before visiting.

Once you add national parks, safaris, whale watching, diving, guided tours or other activities, these costs can become a noticeable part of your overall budget.

A good approach is to decide which experiences are really important to you and include those in your budget from the beginning.

Cash, cards and
Sri Lankan rupees

The local currency is the Sri Lankan Rupee (LKR).

Cards are widely accepted in hotels, larger restaurants, shops and other tourist-oriented businesses, but it is still useful to carry some cash, particularly when travelling outside larger towns.

You can exchange foreign currency at the airport, banks and authorised money changers. Sri Lanka Tourism reminds visitors that money-changing businesses must hold the appropriate permit, so use established and authorised providers.

Exchanging a small amount at the airport can be convenient when you first arrive and need local currency immediately. You can then compare rates and exchange more later if necessary.

Don’t feel that you have to exchange all your money at once. Your needs will depend on where you are travelling and how you plan to pay.

Keep your banknotes clean

If you’re bringing foreign currency to exchange, choose clean, undamaged notes. Even small marks or stains can sometimes make a banknote difficult to exchange.

I learned this myself when two of my US$100 bills were refused because of marks that were barely noticeable to me.

The same principle applies to Sri Lankan rupees: damaged or heavily soiled notes may not be accepted for normal transactions.

It’s a small detail, but checking your banknotes before your trip can save you an unpleasant surprise when you try to exchange them.

Tipping

Tipping in Sri Lanka is not a strict rule, and practices can vary.

In restaurants, check your bill first. A service charge may already be included, particularly in hotels and tourist-oriented restaurants. If it is included, you do not have to add another tip.

If there is no service charge and you are happy with the service, leaving something extra is a nice way to show your appreciation.

The same principle applies to drivers, guides and other people who provide particularly good service.

There is no need to feel pressured to tip everywhere. If someone has made your experience better and you are in a position to do so, a small gesture is always appreciated.

How to keep your costs down

You don’t have to travel like a backpacker to keep your Sri Lanka trip affordable.

A few choices can make a noticeable difference:

  • Eat local. Local restaurants and everyday Sri Lankan food are generally much cheaper than Western-style or hotel dining.
  • Use public transport when it makes sense. Trains and buses can save a lot compared with private transfers.
  • Mix accommodation styles. You don’t have to stay in the same type of property for your entire trip.
  • Choose your paid experiences carefully. You don’t need to visit every attraction just because it is on a list.
  • Compare private transfer offers. Different companies can quote different prices for the same route, so it is worth asking for a few offers before booking a longer journey.

What does a realistic trip budget look like?

Rather than trying to find one exact number for everyone, think about the kind of traveller you are.

Budget traveller

Guesthouse or hostel, local food, public transport and mostly free or inexpensive activities.

Mid-range traveller

Comfortable private accommodation, a mixture of local and tourist restaurants, a combination of public and private transport, and a selection of paid attractions and activities.

Comfortable traveller

Boutique hotels or resorts, private drivers, more restaurant meals, organised excursions and a wider range of paid experiences.

Two people travelling together can also reduce the cost per person, especially when sharing a private room or vehicle.

The good thing about travelling on a budget in
Sri Lanka

You don’t have to spend a lot to have a memorable trip.

Some of the best parts of Sri Lanka cost very little: watching the sunset from the beach, walking through a local town, taking a train through the hills, swimming in the ocean or simply sitting down for a plate of rice and curry.

And if there is something you really want to do — a safari, a beautiful boutique hotel, a private transfer or a special excursion — you can build your budget around those experiences.

Sri Lanka doesn’t have to fit one particular travel budget. You can shape the trip around yours.

Important

Prices in Sri Lanka can change over time, and costs vary by location, season, exchange rate and travel style. Use the figures in this guide as general estimates rather than fixed prices, and check current prices directly with accommodation providers, transport companies and attractions before booking.
