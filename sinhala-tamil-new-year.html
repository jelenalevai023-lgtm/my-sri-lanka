Celebrating the Sinhala & Tamil
New Year.

I had never experienced a New Year quite like this.

There was no midnight countdown. No fireworks lighting up the sky at twelve. Instead, the new year arrived in the middle of a bright April day, with sunshine, beaches and families spending
time together.

And somehow, it felt completely natural.

A new year by the sea

I remember seeing families gathered together, enjoying the holiday and spending time outdoors.

The beach felt different that day.

There was a sense that people weren’t rushing anywhere. They were simply together — talking, eating, relaxing and enjoying the day.

For someone used to New Year’s Eve meaning a cold night, a countdown and fireworks at midnight, seeing people celebrate in the sunshine in April felt wonderfully strange.

New Year in April? Why not.

Not everything needs
to be explained

I didn’t fully understand every ritual or know the meaning behind everything I saw.

And honestly, I didn’t need to.

I could see that this was something deeply rooted in family and tradition. There were customs, food, games and celebrations that had their own rhythm and meaning.

The timing itself was different from what I was used to. The Sinhala and Tamil New Year is traditionally observed according to auspicious times rather than simply beginning at midnight.

That morning, I remember hearing a huge burst of firecrackers.

It was early.

And for a moment I thought: What on earth is happening?

Then I remembered.

It was New Year.

A different way to celebrate

What I liked most was that it didn’t feel like a single moment that everyone was
counting down towards.

It felt more like a whole day belonging
to the celebration.

Families together.

Food being prepared.

Traditional customs being followed.

Children playing.

People visiting one another.

And, of course, plenty of noise and laughter.

I was only an observer, but I still felt welcomed into the atmosphere.

Something unexpected and new

I didn’t come away knowing every tradition.

I didn’t suddenly become an expert on Sinhala and Tamil New Year.

But I experienced something that was completely new to me.

A New Year in April.

In the sunshine.

By the sea.

Surrounded by people celebrating in a way that was familiar to them, but completely new to me.

And maybe that’s one of the best things about travelling.

You don’t have to understand everything to appreciate being part of a moment.
